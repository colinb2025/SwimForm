Film two clips of easy freestyle

  • One from the side and one head-on. We need both: each angle sees what the other can't.
  • A few clean strokes is enough. One easy 50 for the side clip and one length for the head-on clip covers it.
  • Swim the way you normally do: steady pace, normal breathing, no fins, paddles or pull buoy.

{{ mini_diagram("side") }}The side clip

  • Have someone stand on the deck beside your lane, pointing the phone straight across it, not at an angle.
  • Swim down and back. They walk alongside to keep you in the middle of the frame, staying on the same side of the pool.
  • Your whole body in shot, head to feet. No zooming.

{{ mini_diagram("head_on") }}The head-on clip

  • From the end wall, in the middle of your lane, as you swim toward the camera.
  • Hold the phone high and point it down the lane. Looking down sees through the surface; a low camera mostly sees glare.
  • Keep it still, and stop filming before you reach the wall.

Set the phone up right

  • Sideways (landscape), 1080p, and 60 frames a second if your phone offers it. Not slow motion.
  • Upload the original video file. Screen recordings and copies sent through chat apps lose the detail we measure.
  • Pick a quiet lane, so nobody else is in the shot.

Optional: go underwater

  • Above-water clips are all you need. If you have a waterproof pouch or action camera, you can add underwater clips of the same two angles, and we measure the pull directly instead of reading it through the surface.
