{{ mini_diagram("side") }}The side view

  • Film a 50: down the pool, the turn, and back, in one clip. On the way down the camera sees one arm clearly, on the way back the other, so we can check each arm on its own. The turn in the middle is fine; we cut it out.
  • Stand on the deck beside your lane and point the camera straight across it, not at an angle. Stay on the same side of the pool for both lengths.
  • Walk alongside at the swimmer's pace, keeping them in the middle of the frame. A camera that stays still only catches two or three strokes as they pass.
  • Frame the whole body, head to feet, with some water around it. Don't zoom in.
  • Underwater is best: hold the camera just below the surface, still pointing straight across.

{{ mini_diagram("head_on") }}The head-on view

  • Set up at the end wall, in the middle of your lane, with the lens at water level.
  • Start filming when the swimmer pushes off from the far end, and stop before they reach the wall to turn.
  • Keep the camera still. The swimmer coming toward it is what we measure.

What you need

  • A phone, held sideways, filming at 1080p. Use 60 frames per second if your phone offers it, but not slow motion.
  • Someone to film, or a tripod or clamp for the head-on clip.
  • A lane with few other swimmers, so we lock onto you and nobody else.
  • Optional: a waterproof pouch or action camera for an underwater side view.

How to swim it

  • Steady, comfortable pace, the way you'd swim a long set. Not a sprint.
  • Breathe the way you normally do. We check your breathing, so don't hold it.
  • No fins, paddles, pull buoy or snorkel.
  • The two clips can be from different laps.
