How to film your clips

Two short clips of steady freestyle: one from the side, one head-on. A friend with a phone is all it takes. Good footage is the biggest single thing you can do for an accurate report.

What you need

  • A phone, held sideways, filming at 1080p. Use 60 frames per second if your phone offers it, but not slow motion.
  • Someone to film, or a tripod or clamp for the head-on clip.
  • A lane with few other swimmers, so we lock onto you and nobody else.
  • Optional: a waterproof pouch or action camera for an underwater side view. It lets us see the catch and pull, which a camera above the water can't.

The side view

  • Stand on the deck beside your lane and point the camera straight across it, not at an angle.
  • Walk alongside at the swimmer's pace, keeping them in the middle of the frame. A camera that stays still only catches two or three strokes as they pass.
  • Frame the whole body, head to feet, with some water around it. Don't zoom in.
  • Underwater is best: hold the camera just below the surface, still pointing straight across.

The head-on view

  • Set up at the end wall, in the middle of your lane, with the lens at water level.
  • Start filming when the swimmer pushes off from the far end, and stop before they reach the wall to turn.
  • Keep the camera still. The swimmer coming toward it is what we measure.

How to swim it

  • Steady, comfortable pace, the way you'd swim a long set. Not a sprint.
  • Breathe the way you normally do. We check your breathing, so don't hold it.
  • No fins, paddles, pull buoy or snorkel.
  • The two clips can be from different laps.

Things that make clips unreadable

Filming at an angle
A diagonal view distorts every angle we measure. We'll ask you to re-film it.
Screen recordings
Upload the original video. Screen recordings scramble the timing we use to measure your stroke.
Vertical video
A swimmer is long and horizontal. Turn the phone sideways.
Turns and stops
Trim them out. We need 15–30 seconds of continuous swimming.
Other people in shot
Someone on the deck or in the next lane can be mistaken for you.
Sun behind the swimmer
Backlight turns the swimmer into a silhouette. Film with the light behind you.
