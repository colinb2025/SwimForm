{{ mini_diagram("side") }}The side view

  • Film a 50, down and back, in one clip. The turn is fine; we cut it out.
  • Stand on the deck beside your lane and point the camera straight across it, not at an angle.
  • Keep the whole body in frame, head to feet. Don't zoom in.
More on the side view
  • Going down, the camera sees one arm clearly; coming back, the other. That's how we check each arm on its own, so stay on the same side of the pool for both lengths.
  • Walk alongside at the swimmer's pace, keeping them in the middle of the frame. A camera that stays still only catches two or three strokes as they pass.
  • Underwater is the most accurate: hold the camera about half a metre down, still pointing straight across. You can upload an above-water side clip, an underwater one, or both.

{{ mini_diagram("head_on") }}The head-on view

  • Stand at the end wall, in the middle of your lane.
  • Hold the phone high and point it down the lane, and keep it still.
  • Start when the swimmer pushes off the far end. Stop before the wall.
More on the head-on view
  • Looking down sees through the surface, where a low camera mostly sees glare. Stand on the wall or the block if you can.
  • If you can film underwater, put the lens below the surface at the wall instead, or add that as a second head-on clip.
  • The swimmer coming toward the camera is what we measure.

Before you start

  • A phone held sideways, filming at 1080p. Use 60 frames per second if it offers it, but not slow motion.
  • Someone to film, or a tripod or clamp for the head-on clip.
  • A lane with few other swimmers, so we lock onto you and nobody else.
  • Swim at a steady, comfortable pace, the way you'd swim a long set. Not a sprint.
  • Breathe the way you normally do. We check your breathing, so don't hold it.
  • No fins, paddles, pull buoy or snorkel.

Two clips is the minimum, one from the side and one head-on, and they can come from different laps. Every extra clip is used. Optional extras: a waterproof pouch or action camera for underwater clips, and a clip-on polarizing filter to cut surface glare.
